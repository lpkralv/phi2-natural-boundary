## 1. What was proved

Take every ordered way of writing a whole number $N$ as $m+n$. Give it weight $\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)$, where $\Lambda$ is $\log p$ on a positive power of a prime $p$ and zero elsewhere. The sum of these weights is $G_2(N)$. The series
$$\Phi_2(s)=\sum_{m,n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)}{(m+n)^s}=\sum_{N\ge2}\frac{G_2(N)}{N^s}$$
converges absolutely for $\operatorname{Re}s>2$, since there are $N-1$ ordered pairs and each weight is at most $\log^2 N$. Egami and Matsumoto showed that under the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) the function extends as a meromorphic function, which can have isolated poles, to $\operatorname{Re}s>1$. They conjectured that it stops at that line. The paper proves that it stops at every point of the line. The same result holds for sums of any fixed number of prime powers, starting with two.

Corollary D gives the precise unconditional result. For every integer $r\ge2$, the series
$$\Phi_r(s)=\sum_{k_1,\ldots,k_r\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(k_1)\cdots\Lambda(k_r)}{(k_1+\cdots+k_r)^s}$$
has a unique meromorphic continuation to $\operatorname{Re}s>r-1$. For every $s_1$ on $\operatorname{Re}s=r-1$, there is no disc about $s_1$ on which a meromorphic function agrees with the continuation at every point of the right half of the disc where the continuation is analytic. This is the paper's meaning of a natural boundary.

Theorem A proves the continuation and boundary from $\mathrm H_\infty$. For every $\delta>0$, only finitely many non-trivial zeros of $\zeta$ may have real part greater than $\tfrac12+\delta$. RH implies this condition. The argument permits multiple zeros and repeated sums of ordinates.

Write $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ for a non-trivial zero, with real part $\beta$, ordinate $\gamma$ and multiplicity $m(\rho)$. Finitely many zeros off the critical line also suffice for $\mathrm H_\infty$. The hypothesis allows infinitely many off-line zeros whose real parts approach $\tfrac12$ at large heights. Two zeros to the right of the critical line give a sum in the continuation half-plane. In particular, $(\rho,\bar\rho)$ gives the real candidate $2\beta\in(1,2)$ if $\beta>\tfrac12$. Away from $2$ and the points $1+\rho$, a candidate is a pole exactly when the coefficients of the pairs with that sum add to a nonzero number (Proposition 4.2(c)). Under RH every pair sum has real part $1$.

Under RH, Corollary B gives the only poles and their residues. For $\Phi_2$ these are $2$, with residue $1$, and $1+\rho$, with residue $-2m(\rho)/\rho$, where $\rho$ is a non-trivial zero of multiplicity $m(\rho)$. For $\Phi_r$ they are $r$, with residue $1/(r-1)!$, and $r-1+\rho$, with residue $-r\,m(\rho)\Gamma(\rho)/\Gamma(r-1+\rho)$. All are simple. The corollary also gives the limits along the normal to the boundary. Let $\mu\{\tau\}$ be the sum in equation (2) of $m(\rho)m(\rho')\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(\rho')$ over ordered pairs whose imaginary parts add to $\tau$. Then the limit of $\eta\Phi_r(r-1+i\tau+\eta)$ as $\eta\downarrow0$ is $\binom r2\mu\{\tau\}/\Gamma(r-1+i\tau)$ for real $\tau\ne0$. For every real $\tau$, $\eta H(1+i\tau+\eta)$ tends to $\mu\{\tau\}/\Gamma(1+i\tau)$, where $H=\Phi_2-M$. Here $M(s)=2\psi(s-1)/(s-1)-1/(\Gamma(s)(s-2))$ and $\psi=-\zeta'/\zeta$. The atom sums converge absolutely by Lemma 3.4. A pair of distinct zeros contributes in both orders and a diagonal pair contributes once. Under RH, $H$ is holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}s>1$. At $\tau\ne0$ the main term $M$ is analytic at $1+i\tau$, so the limits for $H$ and $\Phi_2$ agree. At $\tau=0$, the limit of $\eta\Phi_2(1+\eta)$ is $\mu\{0\}-2\log(2\pi)$, because $M$ contributes its residue $2\psi(0)=-2\log(2\pi)$ at $1$ (Remark 8.3.1).

Theorem C assumes $\mathrm H_\infty$ and asks what a zero off the critical line would force. Let $B$ be the supremum of the real parts of the non-trivial zeros. If a zero lies off the line, then $\tfrac12<B<1$ and finitely many zeros attain $B$, with at least one attaining it. The real point $2B$ is a simple pole with residue
$$\frac{1}{\Gamma(2B)}\sum_{\operatorname{Re}\rho=B}m(\rho)^2|\Gamma(\rho)|^2>0.$$
The pairs that sum to $2B$ have the form $(\rho,\bar\rho)$ with real part $B$. Each ordered pair contributes a positive term. A conjugate pair contributes twice because the sum includes both zeros. The interval $(1,2)$ excludes the pole at $2$ from Corollary B(i). Under $\mathrm H_\infty$, RH holds exactly when there is no pole in the real interval $(1,2)$. With RH established in the paper's account, the off-line premise does not arise and the interval has no pole.

Theorems A and C and Corollary B are checked in Lean as implications with the premises described in Section 10. Corollary D is proved in the paper by a written argument. It applies Alpöge's certified theorem to Corollary B and uses RH to supply $\mathrm H_\infty$ for Theorem A. The paper's floating-point examples are numerical illustrations.

## 2. Why it matters

The series appears in Egami's 1999 paper. Egami and Matsumoto proved continuation under RH. Their proof of the boundary also assumed (B), which gives an exponentially decaying lower bound on the distance between a nonzero sum of ordinates and any sum from a different pair. This implies (A), uniqueness up to order of each nonzero sum. Hypothesis (A) is a special case of the conjectured linear independence of the positive ordinates over the rationals.

Bhowmik and Schlage-Puchta proved the boundary with RH and (A). Under RH they also stated the equivalence of the boundaries of all $\Phi_r$ with that of $\Phi_2$, and used a finite computation to show singularity at the single point $2\rho_1$, twice the first zero. Their result for $\mathrm H_\infty$ assumes a form of (B) for all zeros. They prove density of the pair sums without RH. For the remaining steps they refer to results of Egami and Matsumoto proved under RH. Table 1 in Section 9 compares the hypotheses of these statements.

Tanigawa and Zhai stated a half-plane form of the boundary under RH for a class containing $\Phi_2$ and $\Phi_r$. The present paper agrees with Egami and Matsumoto's assessment of the proof. Establishing meromorphy on a half-plane and density of the sums of poles of the factors leaves a step open. Contributions from several pairs at the same point can sum to zero. Corollary B(ii) gives the boundary for $\Phi_2$ under RH. Theorem A(iii) gives the boundary for every $\Phi_r$ with $r\ge2$ under $\mathrm H_\infty$, and Corollary D gives it unconditionally. The paper reports finding no earlier complete proof of the $\Phi_2$ boundary under RH alone or without RH.

Peter's continuation theorem for Dirichlet series attached to polynomials requires each auxiliary series to have finitely many poles in each vertical strip of finite width. Here the auxiliary series is $-\zeta'/\zeta$, which has infinitely many poles in the critical strip. Egami and Matsumoto's later work restates the conjecture as open and gives a function-field analogue. The analogue extends to the whole plane when the two fields have the same characteristic, and has the natural boundary $\operatorname{Re}s=2$ when the characteristics differ. Matsumoto's survey gives the boundary for $\Phi_2$ under RH and linear independence. The analogues of Rüppel in residue classes, Cantarini, Gambini and Zaccagnini for Liouville weights, and Zaharescu and Zaki for a related weighted series all impose conditions limiting coincidences.

The new proof chooses points whose representations all use zeros of large ordinate in modulus. The Gamma products for pairs near the lowest height have positive real parts after the same rotation. Exponential decay, together with the bound on the number of zeros per unit of height, keeps all the higher pairs together from cancelling that contribution. Only finitely many points in a bounded range of imaginary parts have a representation using a zero below a fixed height, so the proof can discard those points. In the finite-off-line case the limit along the normal picks out the coefficient at the chosen point, even if other sums are arbitrarily close.

Under $\mathrm H_\infty$, if a zero lies off the critical line, Theorem C(i) proves that the known candidate $2B$ is a simple pole and evaluates its residue. The RH result used in Corollary D rules out that antecedent. Section 9 credits Bhowmik, Halupczok, Matsumoto and Suzuki with the earlier criterion through the poles at $1+\rho$. Under $B<1$, the infimum of the $\sigma\ge\tfrac32$ for which $\Phi_2(s)-1/(s-2)$ is analytic to the right of $\sigma$ is $1+B$. This characterizes RH by analyticity on $\operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac32$. Since $\mathrm H_\infty$ implies $B<1$, Theorem C(ii) gives a new form of the criterion under a stronger hypothesis. Bhowmik and Schlage-Puchta's single singularity at $2\rho_1$ is a precedent on the boundary line. The pole at $2B$ in Theorem C is inside the half-plane.

## 3. How it was found

The author directed an AI research agent through questions about natural boundaries for Goldbach series over primes and prime powers, then chose to tackle the conjecture without an additional hypothesis on ordinates. The acknowledgement says the mathematics is largely the work of AI models under the author’s direction, including phase alignment of pairs with opposite-sign ordinates and the reduction for $\Phi_r$.

Section 1.3 traces five steps. Proposition 3.7 separates a series over ordered pairs of zeros from terms that already extend across the line. Section 4 proves local finiteness of the pair sums in the open right half-plane. Section 5 controls cancellation at selected points. Section 6 uses interior poles if infinitely many zeros lie off the line and radial limits of the on-line pair contribution if only finitely many do. Section 7 reduces the boundary for $\Phi_r$ to the boundary for $\Phi_2$ by Mellin transforms. Theorem C reuses the decomposition and continuation at the single point $2B$.

The proof uses two consequences of $\mathrm H_\infty$. The bound $B<1$ gives a positive margin for the phase cone and a real-part cutoff for the Mellin reduction. The approach of high zeros to the line gives local finiteness in the continuation half-plane and lets interior poles accumulate at the boundary in the infinite-off-line case. A fixed zero-free half-plane supplies only the first consequence. Section 9 also explains why zero-density estimates, whose bounds grow with height, do not give the finiteness required by $\mathrm H_\infty$. Corollary D obtains that finiteness from RH.

Section 1.4 credits earlier work for the continuation under RH, the dominated-convergence argument for the radial limit, and comparisons of the lowest pair with higher pairs. It identifies the new work as the particular normalization and direct proof of the decomposition, the selection of points with only high representations and the phase cone for unequal real parts, the continuation and boundary under $\mathrm H_\infty$ alone, the regularized radial limit including the real point, the Mellin reduction under that hypothesis, and the positive-residue pole at $2B$.

The paper also credits Littlewood's gap theorem for the density of pair sums and Egami and Matsumoto and Bhowmik and Schlage-Puchta for the blow-up criterion. The split into finitely and infinitely many off-line zeros follows Bhowmik and Schlage-Puchta's earlier work on natural boundaries. Bhowmik, Halupczok, Matsumoto and Suzuki gave continuation to $\operatorname{Re}s>2B$ without a hypothesis on the zeros. Earlier identities already write $\Phi_2$ as a sum over pairs plus a remainder. Under $\mathrm H_\infty$, the identity of Cantarini, Gambini and Zaccagnini also gives Theorem A(i) when combined with the continuation argument of Section 4.

## 6. What is still open

The paper leaves the radial limit of the full $\Phi_r$ at $s=r-1$ for $r\ge3$ untreated. Remark 8.3.1 gives the unsubtracted real limit for $\Phi_2$ and explains why the cited argument for positive linear oscillation of the Goldbach mean remainder does not give that conclusion. The paper leaves open in that discussion whether another argument gives the positive oscillation.

In the conditional setting of Theorem C, the proof settles $2B$. It leaves the status of other real candidate pair sums undecided by that argument. For instance, the conjugate pair of a zero with $\tfrac12<\beta<B$ gives the real candidate $2\beta$. The thresholds used to choose high representations are existential, as explained in Remark 8.3.3.

## 7. Limits

The written proof concerns weighted generating series. Section 9 cites Li and Liu's preprint on representations of individual even integers. Their result for all sufficiently large even integers assumes the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet $L$-functions and a further spectral hypothesis.

No human mathematician has checked the proof line by line. The author did not check the Lean code line by line and relies on the kernel and the checks reported in Section 10. Some classical inputs enter the formal theorems as named premises. The paper checks by hand how the cited forms give the required versions of the local zero count, the local logarithmic derivative estimate and Stirling's formula. Appendix A makes the main formal statements available for comparison with the prose.

Section 10 lists material outside the formal verification, including the expository passages, the comparison with earlier work, the remarks in Section 8.3, and the written assembly of Corollary D. The phase example in Section 5 and the estimate of $\mu\{0\}$ in Remark 8.3.1 use floating-point arithmetic. The analytic bound $\mu\{0\}<0.008$ and the negative sign of the full real limit follow without the numerical estimate. Under $\mathrm H_\infty$ the paper uses two boundary arguments because the termwise bound for the radial limit on the critical line can fail when pair sums lie in the continuation half-plane.
